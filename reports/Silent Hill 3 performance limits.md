# Measure SH3’s path to smooth 640×480

**Actual R36S tests identify a workload-dependent graphics and synchronization limit: minimum-setting park gameplay reaches 25.425 FPS at true 640×480 Internal and Output, while three other tested rooms reach approximately 30 FPS.** The park’s current 39.335 ms mean interval must lose **6.002 ms** to reach the 33.333 ms budget; a harder fixed park view needs **10.843 ms**. Automated movement, firing and public-save routes ran on the handheld, with changing live positions, health, ammunition, cache identities, clocks and exit restoration recorded, so these findings do not depend on owner testing. Controlled resolution, GPU-frequency, memory-frequency and Noise Effect comparisons establish real graphics sensitivity; a captured turn hitch also identifies synchronous program setup waiting behind previous presentation work. These are measured limits of current software and configuration, with concrete next changes; they do **not** establish an immutable hardware ceiling. Ordinary GPU execution versus display waiting, process I/O accounting and broader audiovisual/campaign acceptance remain unresolved, so **issue #13 remains open and true-640 smooth 30 FPS remains unpassed**. ([Final measurement record](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/measurement_plan.md), [runtime attribution](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md), [issue #13](https://github.com/ktodorow/sh3-r36/issues/13))

## Four automated routes expose a specific 640×480 gap

Research completed **2026-11-15** against the native Linux ARM64/GLES3 port. The canonical spec requires correct gameplay, original timing, audio, essential effects and repeatable device evidence; an FPS average alone cannot pass it. The current work therefore measures an alive, moving character rather than a title screen or static death scene. Saved host/device scripts deploy isolated seed states, load the selected slot, equip/fire/reload where scheduled, move and turn, collect results, then restore frontend and policies. Public saves extend coverage to mall, hospital and church without overwriting owner progress. They came from Silent Hill Memories’ Beginner-action/Easy-riddle Normal-ending archive, SHA256 `5bf9878a05ec68fb6f09fd88d5db42762c12d9b22ee817936a5507acf948de21`; only save data entered the test seeds. ## Stock clocks and pixel work explain sustained slowdown

The strongest evidence is intervention, not a utilization percentage. In matched earlier fixed-view controls, holding CPU and DMC at their stock maxima while moving GPU from predominantly 400 MHz to 520 MHz improved throughput **20.75%**. Holding CPU/GPU at 1512/520 MHz while reducing DMC from 786 to 528 MHz reduced moving throughput **7.30%**. In a matched `485` 75–180 s moving comparison, Noise Effect Off improved throughput **10.315%**, from 23.45075 to 25.86971 FPS, reducing mean interval **3.988 ms** and shortening the longest below-25 span from 31 to eight seconds. These establish GPU-, memory-frequency- and supported-effect sensitivity in their own contexts. They neither prove DRAM saturation nor predict that combining percentage gains will reach 30. Display Sharp showed no gain; no gain is assigned to Blood Little without a paired contrast. ([Clock, DMC and supported-option contrasts](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/measurement_plan.md), [CPU and swap comparisons](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md))

All three clock domains require attention. Default policies produced lower GPU and DMC frequencies even when CPU looked fast. The new guarded policy experiment selects only supported stock governors, verifies frequencies, journals ownership and restores exact original governors/caps on exit or failure. It does not overclock, change voltage, alter thermal limits or write frequency limits. **That guard remains an opt-in diagnostic; a shipping default performance policy has not been implemented or accepted.** Installed frontend performance-helper commands also have lifecycle and unrelated-side-effect concerns; their presence is not proof that game clocks remain high. A future launcher policy must own the actual game lifetime and pass restoration tests rather than assume the frontend did it. Final device checks verified all three restores, original source, 59 firmware-library hashes, frontend return and no remaining game PID. Five-second telemetry cannot exclude every shorter clock or thermal transition. ([Governor ownership, actual clocks and restoration](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/measurement_plan.md), [launcher context](../scripts/README.md))

The exact minimum-profile `173` cache matrix isolates resolution more tightly. Each warm case used only its corresponding cold case’s generated caches, with non-cache seed bytes unchanged. Resolution changes throughput while mean producer CPU stays approximately **27.5–27.8 ms** and worker CPU approximately **10.8–10.9 ms**. Warm 640 spends approximately **33.04 ms wall / 0.45 ms CPU** inside SDL swap; warm 320 spends **13.14 ms wall / 0.43 ms CPU** there. Final moving 640 similarly uses approximately **28.39 ms producer CPU, 10.86 ms worker CPU and 28.01 ms swap wall time**. Long swap wall time largely represents blocked/deferred work and scheduling rather than CPU instruction execution. Producer and worker run concurrently; swap CPU is already inside worker CPU. Adding those values would invent a serial frame budget. The fixed warm view’s 44.177 ms interval needs **10.843 ms**, or a 24.55% interval reduction, to reach 33.333 ms. The moving final view needs 6.002 ms, or 15.26%; these are separate optimization budgets, not forecasts. ([Matched CPU/wall joins and budget arithmetic](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md))

Actual hardware counters further narrow the diagnosis. The audited official Arm `libGPUCounters` revision `847a04a1597d132a3746e9c6374d3977cea89a6d` successfully enumerated **236 metrics**, one shader core and two execution engines on the installed legacy driver. Two independent approximately three-second moving samples reported GPU-pending time **71.10% and 72.96%** of wall time, with conditional ALU utilization **73.56%/76.75%** and texture utilization approximately **23%**. Arm’s denominators matter: pending includes memory waiting, ALU/texture measures are conditional and concurrent, and the G31 formula does not take an extra two-engine divisor. These cannot be added or converted into a physical saturation percentage. The short samples, observer effects and remaining non-pending time rule out declaring “GPU maxed out” from them alone. ([Actual capability, formulas and sample limits](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/gpu_counter_probe.md), [Arm G31 counter reference](https://github.com/ARM-software/libGPUCounters/blob/847a04a1597d132a3746e9c6374d3977cea89a6d/docs/mali-g31_counter_reference.html), [Arm derived formulas](https://github.com/ARM-software/libGPUCounters/blob/847a04a1597d132a3746e9c6374d3977cea89a6d/hwcpipe/src/hwcpipe/derived_functions.cpp))

The deployed SDL library closes another misleading explanation. Captured **SDL 2.32.10**, SHA256 `f06e06daaf7681aece218f65fec74e351cc07e1ebf0e280b0d66d5de64241de7`, forces cached swap interval **1**: requests for interval 0 return success but read back effective 1. Consequently, those tests did not disable vsync. Its exact performance timer uses `gettimeofday` at 1 MHz, so earlier attempted RAW-to-SDL counter/frame and audio associations are **withdrawn**; independent counter interval statistics remain valid. New coarse captures record REALTIME, MONOTONIC, RAW and SDL anchors explicitly. A first-hand same-board SDL issue reports KMS asynchronous page-flip `EINVAL`; upstream SDL2 commit `8acf7cfc0f88717e5c036f60f0314bfcafe39189` adds a fallback absent from the captured provider. A controlled port-local SDL diagnostic is therefore justified, but no SDL replacement or performance fix has been proven. Removing the forced interval alone would not establish immediate presentation or eliminate all GPU/display waits. ([Captured source/ELF/timer audit](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/gpu_counter_probe.md), [firmware force-interval patch](https://github.com/christianhaitian/rk3326_core_builds/blob/04a465f9536fe8ced4f1a5cd0c6f284e62b5bc90/patches/sdl2-patch-0001-egl.patch), [same-board first-hand report](https://github.com/libsdl-org/SDL/issues/16174), [SDL2 fallback](https://github.com/libsdl-org/SDL/commit/8acf7cfc0f88717e5c036f60f0314bfcafe39189))

## Warm caches remove compilation but leave queued setup hitches

The cache matrix separates loading from sustained performance. Cold 640 restored no programs, compiled **62 shaders** and linked **31 programs**; warm 640 restored 31 binaries with **zero source compile/link events**. Whole-run producer waits attributed to those cold compile/link calls total approximately **558.6/2289.9 ms**, versus approximately **32.9 ms** for warm binary restore. Those totals include queue waiting and are not pure driver execution time. First-room completion falls from **2.29–2.36 s cold to 0.69–0.71 s warm**, yet warmed 640 remains roughly 22.6–23 FPS. Thus the implemented binary cache solves a substantial first-use problem without solving sustained native-resolution rendering. Warm 320 logs no ALSA recovery; cold 320 has one in the selected warm period near an 86.504 ms completion with a 67.113 ms swap. Timing association does not establish that the swap caused the audio event. Both 640 cache cases log no ALSA recovery. ([Whole-run program census](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md), [loading/audio matrix](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/measurement_plan.md))

A recurring first turn also hitches with warm binaries. Final trace-off park reaches **67.410 ms** at that turn. In a separate coarse capture of the same route, the corresponding completion is **76.017 ms**: producer waits **40.108 ms** for queued `glCreateProgram`, but the worker does not enter its matching chunk until **31.376 ms** after submission, overlapping a previous **32.713 ms swap**. Actual worker `glCreateProgram` stays below the trace’s 1 ms threshold with zero event drops; the following binary restore takes **1.030 ms**. Sixty-seven dispatch chunks and later worker-idle spans expose synchronization and feed bubbles. The only recorded `glReadPixels` is a **30.092 ms loading event at 51.074 s**, well before the approximately 82 s turn. Calling this a 40 ms shader compile or a turn-time readback would target the wrong mechanism. ([Clock-calibrated queue attribution](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md), [device trace cross-check](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/measurement_plan.md))

The concrete next candidate is **two-stage preparation of cached native GL programs before guest shader metadata becomes ready**. Current ready-key prewarming already exists: 16 of 47 records can prepare upfront, while the remaining 31 wait for guest vertex/pixel shader hashes. Cache records already retain GLSL, binary data and provider fingerprints. Creating/loading/link-checking native handles and preparing uniform lookups earlier could remove these synchronous first-use queue round trips; attaching guest-key and constant-count metadata must still wait for real guest information. This is an **unimplemented extension**, distinct from repeating issue #14’s existing prewarm. It needs bounded memory, GL-worker ownership, correct destruction/rebinding and invalidation, rejected-binary fallback and pixel equivalence tests. Drivers can defer work until first draw, so gain remains an experiment rather than a promised recovery of the full hitch. ([Current cache lifecycle and proposed seam](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md))

Warm first-room work also retains approximately **555–557 ms of producer CPU**. Each sampled loading frame has 56 CPU samples, including RGBA channel conversion and anonymous guest string-parsing code. Those locations support targeted conversion/loading investigation; they do not prove semantic decompression or exclude I/O. During warmed drawing, a sampled census of approximately **369 draws and 140,090 bytes of vertex/index data per frame** is modest in byte volume but excludes texture, uniform and state traffic. Existing active-prefix uniform comparisons already suppress repeated uploads. Subrange tracking, batching or conversion caches should be chosen only after measuring redundant work at those boundaries. ([Loading-frame samples and upload scope](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md))

## Observer costs prevent a false hardware-ceiling claim

The first full per-command trace was too expensive for baseline use. On `173`, tracing reduced matched moving throughput from **23.722 to 16.438 FPS** and raised worker CPU approximately **18.625 ms per frame**. Clock reads on every asynchronous command caused material cost even when few events survived threshold filtering. Final `becb` coarse mode removes those asynchronous clocks: worker CPU rises only approximately **0.204 ms** relative to its trace-off control, but measured FPS still falls **6.03%**, from 25.42486 to 23.89162. Route, temperature and preflight-history differences complicate that single pair. Both event lanes have zero drops, but **coarse tracing is not established as zero-overhead**, and its FPS must not replace the trace-off baseline. ([Observer controls and preserved failures](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md), [matching final device cases](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/measurement_plan.md))

The final coarse-plus-`glFinish` test is useful attribution only. FPS becomes **13.83967**, mean interval **72.26380 ms**, while pre-swap finish takes **28.65982 ms wall / 0.24240 ms CPU** and following swap falls to **0.30003 ms wall / 0.21875 ms CPU**. This relocates substantial deferred graphics-completion waiting before Swap. It does not measure pure GPU execution: `glFinish` waits for preceding work, and this diagnostic also changes buffering and scheduling. More decisively, existing pacing anchors its deadline at the post-drain swap start, adding approximately **33.077 ms** of sleep per joined completion versus **0.678 ms** in coarse-only mode. The extra **32.399 ms** accounts for almost all observed interval increase. ([Exact event accounting and source mapping](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md), [Khronos completion contract](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/OpenGL-Refpages/main/es3.0/glFinish.xml))

| Mean phase in perturbed final drain capture | Wall time per joined completion |
| --- | ---: |
| Worker command dispatch | 10.065 ms |
| Diagnostic `glFinish` wait | 28.660 ms |
| Following SDL swap | 0.300 ms |
| Existing pacer | 33.077 ms |
| Worker idle | 0.035 ms |
| Approximate phase total / measured completion interval | **72.137 / 72.264 ms** |

Boundary grouping and next-interval pacing explain why phase averages are approximate; nested command scopes and overlapping producer CPU must not be added again. **13.84 FPS is neither a shipping regression nor a 72 ms physical GPU floor.** The combined experiments identify deferred graphics completion and queue scheduling as critical, while exact ordinary GPU-versus-display/GBM/page-flip shares remain unseparated. Useful next instrumentation must preserve or account for pacing, calibrate clocks and measure an observer-off control. A low-overhead display-stage diagnostic or valid disjoint-timer-query capture would answer a narrower question than forcing completion every frame. ([Drain interpretation and remaining attribution](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md), [GPU query validity requirements](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/EXT/EXT_disjoint_timer_query.txt))

Memory does not show a capacity ceiling in sampled routes: one hard-room run peaks at **317,440 KiB RSS, approximately 310 MiB**, while at least **552,752 KiB, approximately 540 MiB**, remains available; warm major-fault counts stay stable. That does not account for all GPU allocations or prove campaign-long bounded growth. Process I/O is a genuine measurement gap: `/proc/PID/io` and even `/proc/self/io` are absent for both normal user and confirmed root, while other process files work. Kernel-config evidence is unavailable, so the exact compile flag is unproved. Low sampled system-wide I/O wait cannot clear process loading I/O. ([Memory, faults and root capability audit](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/measurement_plan.md))

An unrelated diagnostic failure also received a bounded explanation. A coarse-run attempt failed **before game execution** when storage reached zero available blocks; it was not a native runtime failure. Saved recovery scripts validated 20 exact task-owned redundant payload executables against retained immutable packages and removed **644,664,432 bytes**, preserving installed runtime, owner saves, run directories and logs. Available space recovered to **631,424 KiB**. New history references avoid repeatedly copying completed launch evidence. Final runs completed successfully, exact restores passed and frontend remained active. This storage recovery affects workflow reliability, not an FPS optimization claim. ([Failure, verified cleanup and final exit evidence](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/measurement_plan.md), [repeatable host workflow](../scripts/README.md))

## Next changes must remove six to eleven milliseconds

The next work should preserve **true 640×480** and attack separate sustained and first-use costs. The measured interventions already establish which controls are worth keeping stable; they do not justify applying another general cache or indiscriminate CPU rewrite. Sampler/state/attribute/program/uniform caches, ordered GL worker, owned asynchronous uploads/deletes, DISCARD front-image rotation and copy removal, immutable FBO cache, binary shader cache, ready-key prewarm, release-before-pacer behavior and `-O3`/bounded LTO are **already implemented**. Repeating them as new recommendations would ignore the runtime being measured. ([Implemented optimization history](issue-14-renderer-caches.md), [current renderer audit](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/gpu_renderer.md), [current runtime attribution](../research_notes/Silent%20Hill%203%20performance%20limits/cpu_runtime.md))

| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve verified stock performance and supported profile | Make game-lifetime clock policy explicit, with the proven ownership/restore guard; persist native minimum options without silently changing requested dimensions. | Lower GPU/DMC clocks and Noise On demonstrably cost throughput. Current guard is diagnostic. Accept only after normal launcher and failure exits restore exact policies and true-640 tests retain correct effects/audio. |

Accept with a behavioral RED–GREEN–REFACTOR slice, rejection/lifecycle/pixel checks and observer-off route tails; warming that only moves a stall into live gameplay fails. |

## Conclusion

The useful unit of progress is now a **verified reduction in critical-path milliseconds for a named scene**, accompanied by fewer live hitches and intact gameplay. Easy-room success and hard-room failure can coexist on this hardware; that makes a heterogeneous automated regression set more informative than either a single impressive FPS average or a global impossibility claim. Program readiness and sustained graphics cost also need distinct acceptance: eliminating a first-turn queue bubble will not automatically remove the fixed view’s eleven-millisecond deficit.

Issue #13 explicitly allows profiling to pass while 30 FPS remains unpassed, but that does not waive its requirement to separate performance domains. The current evidence supports a concrete research handoff with identified costs and ranked experiments; **ordinary GPU/display shares and unavailable process I/O prevent an unqualified all-criteria measurement pass**. Keep #13 open under its current acceptance wording. Changes remain local and uncommitted for review; future claims should use the replayable controls already established here, without requiring the owner to operate the device.
